# Publication Policy

**Status:** publication of complete methods is frozen as of 2026-09-09.

This repository is the public evidence layer of the TrustData Framework. It may explain what problems are addressed, why the approach matters, and what a synthetic outcome can look like. It is not the distribution channel for the complete consulting method.

## Permitted public content

- High-level problem statements, principles, phases, and domain descriptions
- Synthetic, aggregate outcomes that cannot be traced to a real person or organization
- Screenshots or diagrams that demonstrate communication and analytical capability
- Limited illustrative examples that do not reconstruct a complete method
- Clear statements of limitations and whether an artifact is proposed, tested, or deployed

## Protected content

The following content must remain outside the public portfolio unless an explicit exception is approved:

- Complete questionnaires, assessments, interview guides, and workshop scripts
- Scoring formulas, weighting, thresholds, validation rules, and decision logic
- Reusable templates, checklists, playbooks, acceptance criteria, and delivery guides
- Detailed control-level datasets or data models that disclose the operating method
- Editable dashboard sources, semantic models, binaries, automation, and scripts that expose protected logic
- Real names, email addresses, responses, evidence, client or employer information, system details, credentials, or security-sensitive material

## Release gate

Every future publication must have:

1. An owner and version.
2. A `PUBLIC` classification after privacy, confidentiality, security, and intellectual-property review.
3. Synthetic-data and re-identification checks where data is included.
4. A license and third-party component review.
5. Explicit approval by the repository owner.

Anything not approved through this gate remains `INTERNAL`, `PROPRIETARY`, or `PUBLICATION-BLOCKED`.

## License boundary

Original portfolio content is subject to the root copyright notice unless a narrower file-level notice states otherwise. Third-party components keep their original terms. A future reusable code sample, if intentionally released, must be isolated and licensed separately rather than changing the terms for the complete portfolio.

The interim license architecture requires review by a qualified lawyer before it is treated as a final legal position.
